Measured, 5x5, 60-move scrambles:

  slot-locked, fixed slot order, depth 5     44 of 54 (four faces), 85 s/try

    no locking, depth 3                      44-48 of 54, 3.7 s/try   <-- best

What is left is the genuine endgame: the final 6-10 centre pieces need longer
sequences than a depth-3/4 search reaches. This is the same place human methods
get hard, and it is what a commutator catalogue exists to solve. It is NOT the
same failure as the first attempt — that one could not finish the second face.

Notes on what did and did not help:
  * Locking every correct slot over-constrains: accidentally-correct pieces get
    frozen and block every commutator. Reached only 33-40.
  * Forcing a fixed slot order means always ending a face on its hardest case.
    Letting the search take whichever slot is cheapest is better.
  * Best combination so far: no locking at all, require a net gain in correct
    slots across the whole cube, depth-3 IDDFS with a conjugated-commutator
    fallback.

NEXT: a canonical commutator catalogue for the endgame. Pick a buffer slot,
find a short setup mapping the target onto a helper slot with a known 3-cycle,
apply setup + commutator + inverse setup. Bounded and terminates by

THE METHOD (from Feliks Zemdegs' beginner 5x5 guide, cubeskills.com)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the specification to implement. It is a teaching method with named
stages, which is what this app needs — not an optimal solver.

Centre piece types (CANNOT be interchanged with one another):
  * middle centres — fixed, one per face, define that face's colour
  * corner centres — 4 per face
  * edge centres   — 4 per face
The first model treated all centres as interchangeable by colour. They are
three separate orbits.

Centres are built as 1x3 BARS, not piece by piece:
  1. Build the inner 1x3 bar: two edge centres plus the fixed middle.
  2. Build the two outer 1x3 bars: two corner centres plus an edge centre.
  3. Attach the outer bars to the inner bar.
  4. Solve the opposite face the same way without breaking the first.
  5. Hold the finished pair on L and R, then solve two ADJACENT centres.
  6. Last two centres: same bar approach again.

Edge pairing, first 8 edges — Freeslice:
  Use one slice as a working zone; wide turns around that axis connect wings to
  midges. Store finished edges in the top and bottom layers (4 each), then
  slice to restore the centres.
    R U' R'             insert top-front to front-right, preserving orientation
    F R' F' R           insert, changing orientation
    R U R' F R' F' R    flip the front-right edge in place

Edge pairing, last 4 edges — slice-flip-slice (preserves centres):
    Uw' (R U R' F R' F' R) Uw
    Dw' y' (R U R' F R' F' R) Dw

Edge parity (only on the 12th edge; 5x5 has NO PLL parity):
    Rw U2 x Rw U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Lw U2 3Rw' U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Rw'
  NOTE: this contains an x whole-cube rotation, and the slice-flip-slice above
  contains y'. The engine has no rotation moves. Either add x/y/z to the engine
  or rewrite these in fixed notation. Decide this before implementing edges.

3x3 stage: outer layers only. The existing 3x3 solvers apply unchanged.

2. js/cube/centres-model-nxn.js — compact centre model: centres as a flat
   colour vector with a precomputed permutation per move, so a search step is
   one array gather instead of cloning 98 cubies. Validated at 0 divergences
   over 300 random 25-move sequences. Correct and worth keeping.

TWO STRUCTURAL FACTS (confirmed empirically)
--------------------------------------------
* An outer face turn NEVER moves a centre piece between faces — it only rotates
  that face's own centre block. Outer turns are alignment only, and are free
  once a face is complete.
* Centres migrate between faces ONLY through inner slices. Every insertion is
  therefore necessarily align / slice in / park / slice back — a commutator.
* Solving L then R beats U then D: with L and R done, all three x-slices are
  free and move pieces only among U, F, D, B.

WHERE IT STALLS — the one number that matters
----------------------------------------------
The FIRST centre solves reliably with depth-3/4 IDDFS.
The SECOND centre consistently stalls at 6-8 of 9 with the first locked.
Same wall every attempt:

  plain IDDFS depth 4                       first face ok, second stalls
  commutator templates only                 stalls on the FIRST face
  IDDFS depth 3 + templates + depth 4       first ok, second stalls 6-8/9
  18,468 conjugated commutators, 1-move
    setups, opposite-pair-first order       first ok, second stalls 6-8/9

Verified a genuine limit, not a bug: at a stalled state, an exhaustive sweep of
every [slice, face-turn, slice'] and every [s,b,s',b'] commutator finds nothing
that gains a piece while keeping the first face complete.

The common flaw in all four attempts: treating each step as "strictly increase
the count on the target face". That works while a face is mostly empty and
breaks for the last few pieces, where the correct sequence passes through a
state no better by that measure. This is exactly why the real method builds
BARS — the bar is the unit that makes the last pieces placeable.

NEXT STEPS, in order
--------------------
1. Model the three centre orbits separately. A corner centre can only ever
   occupy a corner-centre slot.
2. Make the unit of work a 1x3 bar, not a single piece. Target "build this
   specific bar in this specific slot", giving the search a reachable goal
   rather than a count to maximise.
3. If still short, widen setups to two moves (~330k shapes). A few seconds per
   insertion is acceptable for a one-off solve.
4. Only then start edge pairing. It assumes solved centres.

Do not resume by trying another variation of count-maximising search. That road
has been walked four times.
